## A. Overall verdict

The theoretical background is unusually honest and, for the primer side, defensible. Every rule names a source, the uncertain ones are labelled, and the tool reports classes rather than predicted Cq values — which is the right call given that the primary sources disagree by master mix. The genome-locator design (seed chains, flank anchoring, copy rules) is well engineered and, notably, the thresholds were calibrated against shuffled decoys rather than guessed. The Otwell 2025 retrospective comparison (section 11 of docs/MISMATCH_CLASSES.md) is more wet-lab grounding than almost any comparable open-source tool has.

Two structural weaknesses limit the science. First, the evidence base for the primer rules is narrow: essentially two papers (Stadhouders 2010, Lefever 2013) plus one validation set, while a 35-year literature on mismatch *identity* and polymerase extension kinetics is absent, and that literature contradicts the encoded Table 1 in places. Second, the probe rules — which decide the fate of every TaqMan assay the tool is built for — are explicitly expert judgement with no source, yet published quantitative probe-mismatch data do exist and were not used. The ΔTm metric is sound as information only but is computed in a way that understates mismatch cost for probes.

## B. Strengths

- **Class-not-number design.** Refusing to predict ΔCq is correct: Stadhouders' own data vary up to sevenfold between mixes, and Otwell used one deliberately permissive condition set (55 °C annealing, 900 nM primers, 50 cycles). Any numeric predictor would be false precision.
- **Provenance discipline.** `oligo/grade.py` carries the source in every `Grade.note`, flags interpolation (position −4 untested), and marks ours-not-theirs encodings. This is better practice than most published tools.
- **The locator is the real novelty.** Nearest-block oligo placement rather than a single offset, signed coordinates for contig-cut copies, N-tolerant seeds, the 12-mer fallback for divergent copies, and per-copy judging with "best copy wins" address failure modes that BLAST-based competitors (PCR_strainer, Simulate_PCR) silently mis-handle. The measured justifications (decoy chains ≤18 anchored bases; Legionella look-alikes at identity 0.60–0.66 with 0 flank bases) are the right kind of evidence.
- **Honest denominators.** "Present, not locatable", "hidden by N", "cut by a contig end", "possibly unassembled" are counted separately and the rationale states what the percentage would be if all were escapes. Most surveillance pipelines just drop these records.
- **Search-sensitivity accounting.** Deriving the Karlin–Altschul score floor per tier from NCBI's reported λ, K and effective search space (`specificity/reach.py`), and verifying it against observed lowest-reported scores (10/8/7 at E=1e3/1e4/1e5), is a genuinely rigorous treatment of what a BLAST search *could not* have reported. I have seen no other assay-checking tool do this.
- **Collection-date axis** alongside release year is the correct response to submission-lag bias.

## C. Weaknesses and risks, ranked

**1. Mismatch identity is encoded from one source that conflicts with the polymerase literature (high severity).**
Rule R1 puts terminal C–A, A–C, G–T, T–G (group G3) as "acceptable" → `tolerated`. That is Stadhouders' Table 1, but it contradicts two foundational datasets. Huang, Arnheim & Goodman (1992, *Nucleic Acids Res* 20:4567, doi:10.1093/nar/20.17.4567) measured Taq extension of all 3'-terminal mispairs: transition mispairs A·C, C·A, G·T, T·G were extended 10⁻³–10⁻⁴ less efficiently than correct pairs. Kwok et al. (1990, *Nucleic Acids Res* 18:999, doi:10.1093/nar/18.4.999) found A:G, G:A and C:C reduced yield ~100-fold and A:A ~20-fold, but — importantly for your G2 group — mismatches of T with G, C or T had "minimal effect", and C·T was the *easiest* mispair to extend in Huang (~10⁻², the exception). So the literature supports your G1 = avoid, but puts **C–T/T–C far milder than G2 implies**, and suggests **terminal G3 transitions are not safely "tolerated"**. Your doc already notes the Stadhouders/Lefever disagreement on C–T/T–C and takes the worse; the deeper problem is that terminal G3 is scored tolerated on the strength of a single study.
*Recommendation:* add a third axis to R1 from Huang 1992 extension efficiencies and Kwok 1990 yields; where the three sources disagree, grade terminal G3 as `at_risk`, not `tolerated`, and print all three sources. Do not relax a terminal mismatch to `tolerated` on one paper's "acceptable (<2.0 Ct)".

**2. Probe rules have no quantitative basis although data exist (high severity).**
R9 is flagged as expert judgement, correctly, but usable data were available:
- Süß, Flekna, Wagner & Hein (2009, *J Microbiol Methods* 76:316, doi:10.1016/j.mimet.2008.12.003): single mismatches in probe binding regions had *more* pronounced effects on amplification curves than primer mismatches, with quantification error up to 33% (primers up to 63%).
- Klungthong et al. (2010, *J Clin Virol* 48:91, doi:10.1016/j.jcv.2010.03.012): a *single* mismatch at the third base of the WHO swH1 probe reduced sensitivity in 23/37 influenza A(H1N1)pdm09 viruses; homologous probe/primers recovered a mean 4.66 Ct.
- Kutyavin et al. (2000, *Nucleic Acids Res* 28:655, doi:10.1093/nar/28.2.655) — already cited from the abstract — actually gives the mechanism you need: MGB probes are most discriminating when the mismatch lies *in the MGB region* (the 3' end of the probe), and A/T-rich duplexes are stabilised more. Your R9 is explicitly position-free for MGB; Kutyavin says position matters.
- Otwell 2025, which you already use, has 56 probe-site templates including the C4 ORF8 deletion series: deletions ≤6 nt of a 26-nt probe site gave ≤5 Ct shift and no false negatives, 7 nt was worse. That is directly usable for probe gaps, which R5 leaves `indeterminate`.
*Recommendation:* replace position-free MGB grading with a position-aware rule (mismatch in the 3' third of an MGB probe = at least `at_risk`, elsewhere `indeterminate`), cite Kutyavin 2000 and Klungthong 2010, and grade small probe deletions from Otwell's own measurements rather than leaving them `indeterminate`.

**3. Internal mismatches are treated as nearly free; two strong datasets say otherwise (high).**
R2 grades a single mismatch beyond −9 as "almost negligible" from Lefever alone. Against this: Bru, Martin-Laurent & Philippot (2008, *Appl Environ Microbiol* 74:1660, doi:10.1128/AEM.02403-07) — a *single* mismatch in the second half of the primer caused up to **1000-fold** underestimation of copy number. Rejali, Moric & Wittwer (2018, *Clin Chem* 64:801, doi:10.1373/clinchem.2017.282285) — extension rates were still reduced by up to 84% with a C·C mismatch six bases from the 3' end, and mismatch effects depend on local sequence, KCl and polymerase type. Whiley & Sloots (2005, *J Clin Virol* 34:104, doi:10.1016/j.jcv.2005.02.010) — as few as **two** mismatches in a single primer raised Ct and distorted viral load.
*Recommendation:* keep `tolerated` for a single distal mismatch but attach a mandatory quantification caveat citing Bru 2008 (quantitative assays may still be badly wrong), and consider `at_risk` for a C–C mismatch anywhere in the 3'-most ~8 nt (Rejali). Also state that the classes concern *detection*, not quantification — a lab using the assay for viral load has a different risk profile.

**4. The RT step is a documented blind spot (medium-high).** R4 is deliberately not encoded. But Christopherson, Sninsky & Kwok (1997, *Nucleic Acids Res* 25:654, doi:10.1093/nar/25.3.654) show RT-PCR tolerates 2–4 mismatches with little effect while 5–6 in a 28–30-mer cut yield 22–100-fold — i.e. a whole-duplex load effect, not a 3'-end effect, and one your R3/R3b 16-nt window cannot express. Most Dutch clinical virology assays are one-step RT-PCR, so the single Taq-on-DNA basis is the opposite of conservative for reverse primers in some mixes (rTth) and over-strict in others (Taq+MMLV).
*Recommendation:* add a template_type-aware note (not a class) citing Christopherson 1997, and surface total mismatch count over the whole oligo next to the 16-nt count.

**5. The 3'-most-16-nt window is an artefact of Lefever's 20-mers (medium).** R3/R3b/R8 count only within 16 nt of the 3' end, because that is what Lefever tested on 20-mers. Applied to a 27-nt probe-era primer or a 30-mer, the window covers roughly half the oligo and R3b then treats up to 4 mismatches as `tolerated` on the strength of Otwell's handful of 5'-end templates. Christopherson 1997 contradicts this for long primers.
*Recommendation:* make the window relative (e.g. 3'-most 80% of the oligo, capped at 16 nt) or at minimum emit an explicit warning when oligo length > 22 nt that R3b is extrapolated.

**6. ΔTm is computed in a way that is systematically optimistic (medium).** `specificity/duplex.py` calls `thermo.heterodimer(oligo, reverse_complement(subject))` — i.e. it rebuilds a perfect complement of the *subject* and then dimerises, so the mismatch enters only via primer3's dimer search, and the docstring concedes a 3'-terminal mismatch is treated as a dangling end. Using SantaLucia's internal-mismatch nearest-neighbour parameters (SantaLucia & Hicks 2004, *Annu Rev Biophys Biomol Struct* 33:415, doi:10.1146/annurev.biophys.32.110601.141800) directly on the aligned duplex would be more faithful. For MGB and LNA probes the model simply does not apply (You et al. 2006, *Nucleic Acids Res* 34:3409, doi:10.1093/nar/gkl175, show LNA triplets centred on the mismatch change discrimination, and that G–T behaves backwards) — the docs say so, which is good, but ΔTm is still printed for MGB probes, where it is the metric users will lean on hardest.
*Recommendation:* either suppress ΔTm for modified probes or print it with a hard "not applicable to this chemistry" marker; and compute ΔTm from an explicit mismatched-duplex NN calculation, not a reverse-complement dimer.

**7. BLAST word size 7 has a quantified blind spot that is not reported (medium).** Ye et al. (2012, *BMC Bioinformatics* 13:134, doi:10.1186/1471-2105-13-134) state Primer-BLAST with word size 7 misses targets with ≤6 consecutive matches, and estimate ~0.5% of 2-mismatch 20-mer targets are missed for that reason. Your `always_reported()` correctly models the word-size run constraint — but the `floor_findings` text reports only mismatch counts, not this failure mode, and the default E-value is 1000 where Primer-BLAST uses 30,000 for the primer-only case. Your own docs show saturation forces the lower value; that is a defensible trade-off, but the report should say the search is less sensitive than Primer-BLAST's default, not just quote a floor.

**8. Population-proxy claims (medium-low).** The docs handle sampling bias well, but two issues remain unaddressed: (a) NCBI/RefSeq **redundancy** — clonal outbreak clusters and repeat submissions of the same strain inflate "percent detectable" in a direction that cannot be bounded; varVAMP's companion workflow (Johnston et al. 2026, doi:10.64898/2026.09.21.753121) explicitly clusters to reduce redundancy before assessing primers. (b) The one-copy-wins rule for multi-copy targets is correct for detection but means that a genome where 9 of 10 opa copies have escaped reads identical to one where none has — relevant for quantitative assays and for sensitivity near the LoD.
*Recommendation:* report a clustered/deduplicated inclusivity figure alongside the raw one, and report the per-genome fraction of detectable copies, not just the best copy.

**9. Homopolymer rule R5b is invented (low, but honestly flagged).** The class is yours, the cited support (Zhu & Wartell; Tanaka; Elbrecht) is about duplex stability and slippage, not amplification. The dual strict/lenient reporting is the right mitigation. Otwell's deletion data could at least bound the probe case.

**10. "Likely failure" is a strong label (low).** Otwell's own headline is that most SARS-CoV-2 assays were "extremely robust" despite signature erosion; your section 11 shows 9 `likely failure` templates of 132 after the rule change, all delayed ≥3 Ct or missed at 50 copies — so the label is currently well calibrated on that set. But it is one virus, one permissive condition set, with one assay (China_N) dominating the 4-mismatch cases, as you note. Do not let the label harden into a pass/fail; the current "review status, the reviewer decides" framing is correct and should be defended against future feature pressure.

**What is genuinely novel here:** (a) graded, per-rule, source-cited mismatch classes with a published wet-lab cross-check; (b) the chain locator with flank anchoring, nearest-block oligo placement and explicit accounting of contig ends, N runs and multi-copy families; (c) the Karlin–Altschul score-floor reporting plus partner scan, which quantifies what the specificity search could not have seen; (d) the channel model for genus/species multiplexes; (e) the remote-only, no-local-database design aimed at a diagnostic lab's yearly re-evaluation record. I found no tool combining these.

## F. Suggested wet-lab validation

Priorities, in order:

1. **Single MGB probe mismatches by position.** The largest unsupported class (`indeterminate`, R9) and the one that most often decides a genome's fate. Synthetic templates with one mismatch at each position of your actual MGB probes, at 3 copy levels including near the LoD, under the lab's own mix and 60 °C annealing. This directly tests Kutyavin's MGB-region prediction and would convert `indeterminate` into a real class.
2. **Terminal G3 mismatches (C–A, A–C, G–T, T–G).** R1 calls these `tolerated` on one source while Huang 1992 and Kwok 1990 suggest otherwise. Test each on both primers of one real assay. If any costs >2 Ct, regrade the whole G3-terminal cell.
3. **Homopolymer bulges (R5b), using the N. gonorrhoeae poly-A 7→8/9 case.** Entirely unmeasured and it swings your reported inclusivity from 68.9% to 85.7% — the single largest numerical uncertainty in the tool's output. Also sequence a few of those genomes by long reads to establish how many "variants" are platform error.
4. **The EV-D68 C–A at −3 enterovirus reverse primer, on RNA template in your one-step mix.** Tests R1 and the untested RT caveat simultaneously, on a variant affecting 816 of 5,130 records.
5. **Reverse-primer mismatches in your one-step RT-PCR mix** (2 and 3 internal mismatches). Determines whether the Taq-on-DNA basis is conservative or anti-conservative for your actual reagents — Stadhouders' two RT kits behaved oppositely.
6. **Quantification, not just detection.** For any assay used for viral load, test a single internal mismatch and measure the copy-number error, per Bru 2008. The tool currently says nothing about this and labs may assume `tolerated` means "quantifies correctly".
7. **One confirmed off-target product** from the specificity tier, to check the partner scan and the critical-site thresholds against reality.
8. **Software verification for the quality system:** a frozen reference dataset (a few hundred genomes with hand-curated expected classes) re-run at each release, plus the Otwell template set as a regression fixture. This is what the lab needs to verify the software under its own quality system, as the report already says it must.

All of this should feed the existing `evidence:` / LAB rule, which is a good mechanism — lab results overriding in silico classes per site variant is exactly the right architecture for a tool whose rules are this uncertain.
