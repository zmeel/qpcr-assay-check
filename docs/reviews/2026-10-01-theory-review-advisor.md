ADVISOR REVIEW — theoretical background of qpcr-assay-check (read-only; no file changed)

## A. Overall verdict

The methodological core is better than most of what exists in the open-source field, and clearly better than most published in silico re-evaluations. Exhaustive genome-by-genome analysis, one outcome per genome, best copy per genome, whole-fragment (F+probe+R on one copy) as the decisive unit, explicit accounting for every genome that drops out of the denominator, and a measured (not assumed) locator are all sound and defensible in a quality system. The honesty of the documentation is a real asset.

Three things keep it short of "fit to support a regulatory-style claim": (1) the graded classes were tuned on the Otwell data and are then presented as validated against them — that is calibration, not validation; (2) probes, which in a hydrolysis-probe assay decide detection, have essentially no evidence base, and newly available wet-lab data are not used; (3) the headline percentages are printed next to very large unjudged/undetermined fractions (Legionella genus channel: 95.8% of 4,337 judged, with 7,444 undetermined), which comes close to the project's own prohibition on presenting a sample as the population. Also, there is no thermodynamic decision step anywhere, although that is precisely what FDA asks for as stage 1 and what the AOAC in silico recommendations and TNTBLAST-based tools are built on.

## B. Strengths

- Exhaustive, not sampled; every listed genome ends in exactly one documented outcome, and "genomes listed vs judged" is always reconciled. Few published analyses do this (Gand et al. 2021, Genes 12:565, doi:10.3390/genes12040565, use deduplicated sets and report inclusivity percentages without this accounting).
- Whole-fragment outcome on one copy is the right unit: it is the amplicon, not the oligo, that fails. Per-oligo figures demoted to diagnostics is correct.
- BLAST reach/score-floor computation from NCBI's own λ, K and search space, with the measured confirmation (predicted 10/8/7 = observed 10/8/7), plus the "Reported down to" column: this is a genuinely original honesty feature. I know of no comparable tool that quantifies its own blind spot.
- Partner scan and probe re-alignment inside products address the real failure mode of oligo BLAST (short-oligo E-value floor), and the N-as-mismatch fix after the PX731700.1 false product shows good discipline.
- Every rule names its source and says where there is none (R5, R5b, R7, R9). Rare and valuable.
- Locator thresholds derived from shuffled-decoy nulls and real divergent copies, with the miss cases listed.

## C. Weaknesses and risks, ranked

1. Circular validation of the mismatch classes (highest). R3/R3b/the 16-nt counting window were changed on 2026-09-30 after inspecting Otwell outcomes, and section 11 then reports that every likely-failure template shifted ≥3 Ct and no detectable template did. That is in-sample fit; the wiki repeats it as a performance statement. One assay (China_N) dominates the 4-mismatch cases. Recommendation: relabel section 11 "calibration set", report leave-one-assay-out results, and validate on independent data — Knight et al. 2025 (Sci Rep 15:16184, doi:10.1038/s41598-025-98444-8; 15 assay designs, best ML model 82% sensitivity / 87% specificity by tenfold CV), the 90 FMDV oligo templates of GoPrime (Howson et al. 2020, Pathogens 9:303, doi:10.3390/pathogens9040303), and the 3'-terminal yield data of Kwok et al. 1990 (Nucleic Acids Res 18:999, doi:10.1093/nar/18.4.999: A:G/G:A/C:C terminal mismatches ~100-fold yield loss, A:A ~20-fold, T mismatches minimal, double mismatches in the last four bases including the terminal one drastically reduced yield — independent and consistent with R1/R3).
2. Probe rules are the weakest part of the chain and are now out of date. R9 is explicitly expert judgement, yet published and newly available data exist: Süss et al. 2009 (J Microbiol Methods 76:316, doi:10.1016/j.mimet.2008.12.003) found probe-region mismatches more consequential than primer-region ones; Kutyavin et al. 2000 (Nucleic Acids Res 28:655, doi:10.1093/nar/28.2.655) quantifies MGB single-mismatch discrimination; GoPrime measured a complementarity floor around 85% for probes with mean ΔCt ≈ 6.6 and no positional model; Otwell et al. 2025 (Front Cell Infect Microbiol 15:1524025, doi:10.3389/fcimb.2025.1524025) measured probe deletions directly (≤6 nt tolerated; 7 nt pushed Ct above 40 at 50 copies; 8 nt not detected) and observed that probe defects act as a near-binary loss of signal rather than loss of efficiency. The tool grades all probe gaps as indeterminate and all MGB ≥2 mismatches as likely failure position-free. Recommendation: grade probe deletions by length from Otwell's own numbers; add a probe complementarity floor from GoPrime; split the MGB single-mismatch indeterminate by ΔTm against the configured annealing temperature.
3. No thermodynamic decision step; ΔTm/ΔG is information only. FDA's "Policy for Evaluating Impact of Viral Mutations on COVID-19 Tests (Revised)", 12 January 2023 (https://www.fda.gov/media/146171/download) names as stage 1 a Tm calculation with and without the mutation, under the test's own ion and primer concentrations, with the method described, and sets as the decision criterion a Tm reduced to at or below the assay's annealing temperature. SantaLucia et al. 2020, AOAC Appendix Q (J AOAC Int; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8370429/) recommends thermodynamic scoring over BLAST similarity; Gans & Wolinsky 2008 (Nucleic Acids Res 36:e74, doi:10.1093/nar/gkn301) show free energy outperforms Tm as a match criterion. Recommendation: keep classes as the judgement but add a documented thermodynamic gate — flag any site whose predicted duplex Tm falls at or below the annealing temperature — and say in the report that this is FDA's stage-1 criterion. This is cheap, already computed, and closes the largest regulatory gap.
4. Release year is the status axis, collection year "information only" — backwards. FDA's frequency framing is mutations seen above ~5% in at least 2000 sequences over a recent week/month/quarter; CoMIT (Walker, Smith et al. 2025, BMC Bioinformatics 26:51, doi:10.1186/s12859-025-06046-y) stratifies by lineage over three-month windows keyed to collection dates and compares against the previous quarter. Your own enterovirus run showed the problem: the 2025 release year's at-risk bulk was collected in 2022. Recommendation: make collection date primary where present, release year the fallback and a sensitivity analysis.
5. No deduplication or diversity weighting. 41,117 N. gonorrhoeae assemblies in one release year, and Legionella drafts of which 2,850 of 2,912 "from parts" are all-perfect L. pneumophila: the percentage is dominated by sequencing effort. AOAC Appendix Q advises curating inclusivity panels for genetic diversity rather than using exhaustive databases raw. Recommendation: publish, beside the raw count, a figure per unique site haplotype and per submitter/BioProject (and per ST/lineage where NCBI gives it); keep the raw figure as the primary, but let the reader see the effect.
6. Undetermined removed from the denominator can swallow the answer. Neisseria: 3,064 undetermined of ~20,000; Legionella genus channel: more undetermined than judged. Recommendation: refuse a status (INCOMPLETE) when undetermined plus unjudged exceeds a configurable share of listed genomes, and always print the two bracketing percentages (all undetermined detected / all escapes) next to the headline, as is already done for "present, not locatable".
7. Specificity has two rule systems and a residual blind spot. Off-target sites use ≤3 mismatches / ≥5 clean 3' nt heuristics, unrelated to grade.py's evidence-based classes — the same biology judged two ways. The "BLAST reported neither primer" gap is acknowledged but the fix (reference-fragment/amplicon BLAST per off-target tier) is not built. And nothing expresses exclusivity in the convention a regulator will ask about: the FDA molecular diagnostic template defines in silico cross-reactivity as greater than 80% homology between a primer/probe and a sequence of the targeted organism (text reproduced at https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/covid19-industry/medical-devices/testing/antigen-devices/fda-guidance-molecular-diagnostic-template-commercial-manufacturers.html, July 2020). Recommendation: reuse grade.py for off-target priming, build the per-tier amplicon BLAST, add a homology-% column, add the Tm gate.
8. Homopolymer spread makes the headline a configuration choice. Neisseria 68.9% vs 85.7% strict/lenient (later 77.8% vs 93.0%). Recommendation: when the spread exceeds a few points, publish the interval, not one number; add sequencing platform / assembly method from Datasets metadata to the RunLengthBreakdown (BioProject is absent from the store — platform is available in assembly_info and is the more informative field).
9. Genome scan covers only the first locus, while FDA explicitly recommends multi-target redundancy and interpretation rules. For a true multiplex the clinically relevant endpoint — detected by at least one target — cannot be computed. Rank this above cosmetic work.
10. RNA/RT asymmetry not reflected in the status. Stadhouders' kits behaved in opposite directions; your enterovirus assay is RNA graded on Taq-on-DNA. Recommendation: not a mix setting, but for template_type RNA report the rTth/taq_mmlv columns (already documented) as a sensitivity range in the rationale.
11. Dropping run history conflicts with recurring-monitoring expectations (FDA 2023; Gand et al. 2021 argue for regular re-evaluation). A minimal substitute is computable within one run: per-site-variant frequency in the newest window vs the previous one, flagging any variant crossing 5% — FDA's own trigger.
12. Minor: 95/80% have no cited source (fine, they are configurable and labelled as the lab's choice, but should be mapped to the FDA framings in the methods text); R7 counts the best-matching degenerate variant at full weight; "detectable" reads as a prediction — "no effect expected from published data" is more honest.

## D. Gaps versus guidelines

- No Tm/ΔG decision step (FDA 2023 stage 1) and no named escalation path: FDA's sequence is in silico, then synthetic/genomic wet testing, then isolate, with a >3-fold LoD difference triggering risk analysis. The report should name that next step for each at-risk/likely-failure variant above a frequency threshold.
- No frequency-based variant trigger over a recent collection-date window of ≥2000 sequences (FDA's 5% framing).
- Exclusivity not expressed in the >80% homology convention.
- AOAC Appendix Q's advice on sequence quality (full-length over partial, partial sequences segregated) is only partly implemented: the Nucleotide source mixes CDS-only records in, which showed up as 1,089 "region not found" enterovirus records.
- MIQE 2.0 (Bustin et al. 2025, Clin Chem 71:634–651, doi:10.1093/clinchem/hvaf043) is worth citing in the methods section as the reporting frame; I did not verify any in silico-specific clause in it.
- NOT VERIFIED by me: CLSI MM17/MM03 text (no accessible copy) and the operative wording of EU Implementing Regulation 2022/1107 on primer/probe sequence alignments (EUR-Lex returned nothing through this proxy; only secondary summaries). Do not cite either in the report on my authority.

What you do that none of them do: exhaustive NCBI-driven genome listing with incremental storage of only target regions; a divergence-tolerant chain locator with context flanks; explicit reconciliation of unjudged genomes; a computed BLAST score floor; per-channel verdicts; and a lab-evidence override. What they do that you do not: a thermodynamic criterion (PCR_strainer, TNTBLAST, ipcr, GoPrime), quantitative ΔCt prediction (GoPrime, Knight), lineage- and collection-date-based frequency monitoring of multi-target designs (CoMIT).

## F. Five most valuable next steps

1. Add the thermodynamic gate: flag any primer or probe site whose predicted duplex Tm falls at or below the configured annealing temperature, cite FDA 2023 stage 1, and use ΔTm to split the MGB single-mismatch indeterminate class.
2. Rebuild the probe rules on real data (Otwell deletion lengths, GoPrime complementarity floor, Süss, Kutyavin) and stop grading all probe gaps indeterminate.
3. Relabel section 11 as calibration and validate on independent data (Knight 2025, GoPrime's 90 templates, Kwok 1990), reporting leave-one-assay-out results.
4. Make collection date the status axis, add a de-duplicated/diversity-weighted figure beside the raw percentage, and refuse a status when undetermined plus unjudged exceeds a configurable share of listed genomes (always printing the bracketing percentages).
5. Close the specificity gaps: per-tier amplicon BLAST, grade off-target sites with grade.py instead of the parallel heuristic, add an 80%-homology column, and extend the genome scan to every locus so multi-target resilience can be reported.
