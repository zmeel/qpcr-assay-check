# Run

[Assay](../assays/hsv1-gg-typing.md), v2.1.0, the first run, made with the code of before
2026-10-23's fixes (the target tier was still searched; no lineage check of off-target

published design), exclusivity and background (both explained below).

# Specificity

- **HSV-2: amplified, not detected** (8,964 sites, class `amplified_not_detected`). The
  type-common primers bind it, as the paper designed them to, and the HSV-1 probe does not: in
  silico this HSV-1 detection assay does not detect HSV-2. The tier reads Exceeds limit because a
  product with critical primer sites counts against the assay whether or not the probe detects
  it, and for an HSV-1 assay that flag is fair: the assay amplifies HSV-2 and relies on the probe
  alone to ignore it (and in a co-infection the HSV-2 product competes for the primers).
- **Every other exclusivity organism**: no predicted product.
- **Human background**: 31 products the primers could make with 4-5 mismatches each, none
  detectable by the probe; one critical primer site in a product put the tier at Exceeds limit.
- **Incomplete in part**: the partner scan stopped at its 1,000-fetch limit (519 primer sites not
  scanned; 515 products whose probe site was not re-aligned), almost all on HSV-2 records, and
  four oligo variants filled their hit list. A probe site BLAST did not report could be missing
  there; against HSV-2 HG52 the probe has 10 mismatches in 25 nt, so detection is unlikely.
- **Intended target**: "no perfect full-length hit for forward" - the C at the forward primer's
  2nd base, which every genome reads as T (predicted in the assay file); not a problem of the
  target. That search is off by default since the same day.

# Detection

614 of 618 genome-sized records carry the region. **96.1% detectable, 3.9% at risk, 0.0% likely
failure.** 24 escapes, all through mismatches: 20 by the forward primer (most of them the
HM5855xx series of one submission), 4 by the probe (HM585508.2, MH160372.1, MH160384.1,
ON513441.1).

Status **Incomplete**: only 26 genomes with the region were collected 2023-2026 (100% detectable)
against the 100 the setting asks for, and 63.7% of the records have no usable collection year. By
release year: 96.0% detectable of 223 records released 2023-2026.

# Search times

# What it means for the laboratory

As an HSV-1 detection assay it holds in silico: it detects HSV-1, the probe does not detect HSV-2,
and nothing else on the list gives a product. It does not detect HSV-2 at all, which the
laboratory should keep in mind if it ever relies on this PCR for HSV in general. What is open is the inclusivity verdict, for want of dated recent genomes
(`status_axis: release` would decide it on release year instead), and the probe's discrimination
on the HSV-2 products the partner scan did not reach. In silico analysis does not replace
experimental validation; the laboratory verifies the tool within its own quality system.
