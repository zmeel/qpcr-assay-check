So the second group of the suggested panel - the rest of the genus, where conserved SSU rRNA made
cross-reaction likeliest - was the right worry, and it is where the assay is not
species-specific. Whether that matters is the laboratory's call: these are animal species, and
how often they reach a human stool sample is an epidemiological question the tool cannot answer.
Nothing in the faecal differential or among the flagellates outside Giardia gave a
probe-detectable product.

The background tier predicted 31 products and **none detectable by the probe** (*E. coli* 30
records, 8 primer mismatches, 75 bp; human 1, 6 mismatches, 420 bp), so the flora gives
primer-only products. Its hit list saturated for both forward-primer forms, which the report
states, so that evidence is partial.

# Target detection

96.0% detectable of 202 records collected 2023-2026, 98.5% including at risk, 1.5% likely
failure - but **Incomplete**, because 67.7% of the records with the region have no usable
collection year, over the 25% limit. Giardia records rarely carry `collection_date`, so for this
assay `inclusivity.status_axis: release` would put the status on evidence the records actually
have; the collection axis leaves two thirds of them out.
